## Problem

Managers who plan by the quarter often still do it in a spreadsheet. They export
projects and initiatives, arrange and annotate them in rows and columns, compare
options, and then copy the result back into Linear by hand.

The export is detached from the moment it is made. Changes in Linear do not reach the
sheet, edits in the sheet do not reach Linear, and the notes and extra columns added in
the sheet stay in the sheet.

## Proposal

A grid that behaves like a spreadsheet, whose rows are real Linear projects and
initiatives. Keyboard navigation, paste, fill, free columns and, later, formulas work as
they do in a spreadsheet. Linked values such as status, lead, dates and priority stay
Linear's, and edits to them go back to Linear under the name of the person who made
them.

The question it tests is whether a planning team arranges, annotates and compares plans
faster this way than with a plain export or with Linear's own views, without giving up
Linear as the source of truth.

The work is split into four projects, each shipped here and checkable by hand:

1. Workspace mirror and read-only sheet. A copy of the workspace's public-team projects
   and initiatives, kept current by webhooks and a scheduled reconcile, with sign in with
   Linear and two read-only sheets.
2. Sheet document and commit to Linear. Sheets saved as documents, staged edits, a
   commit that writes each change under its author's name, and draft rows.
3. Live multi-user editing. Several people in one sheet, with cell-level conflicts,
   history and undo.
4. Formulas and snapshots. Formula columns, and snapshots that can be compared and
   applied.

The first project is under way. The grid is Handsontable behind an adapter module, so it
can be replaced without touching the rest (REC-004). The server reads the workspace with
a Linear OAuth app's own credentials, scoped by Linear to public teams, and the same app
signs people in (REC-005).
